Represent, evaluate, interpret, solve

Exercise 1 · Area of a square

A square has side length \(s\) inches and area \(A\) square inches. Name the function \(g\): \(A=g(s)=s^2\).

\(g\) names the function. \(s\) is the input. \(g(s)\) is the output.

\(g(4.9)\) means “g of 4.9,” not multiplication.

1a. Represent the areas for side lengths 3.5 in and 26.92 in, without calculating them.

Worked answer

The input goes inside the parentheses: \(g(3.5)\) and \(g(26.92)\) name the two areas before any calculation.

in²

Worked answer

\(g(4.9)=(4.9)^2=24.01\).

Compare your reasoning

When the side length is 4.9 inches, the square’s area is 24.01 square inches. In this positive-side context, area 24.01 in² also identifies side length 4.9 in.

1d. What does solving \(g(s)=81\) ask for?

Worked answer

The output is given. We seek the input: a side length whose square is 81. This reverses the evaluation question.

in

Worked answer

\(s^2=24.01\) gives algebraic roots ±4.9. A side length is positive, so \(s=4.9\).

Compare your reasoning

\(g(5)+23\) is 23 in² more than the area of a square with side 5 in. \(4g(3.4)\) is four times the area of a square with side 3.4 in; it can represent the total area of four such squares.

An expression can be an input

Exercise 2 · Growing square

The square starts with side length 0 and its side increases at 3 inches per second. Let \(t\) be seconds since growth began; \(g(s)=s^2\) still converts a side length to an area.

\(t\ \longrightarrow\ 3t\ \longrightarrow\ g(3t)=(3t)^2=9t^2\)

2a. What does \(3t\) represent?

Worked answer

Rate × time gives side length: 3 inches per second times t seconds is 3t inches.

2b. Which expression is equal to \(g(3t)\)?

Worked answer

Substitute the entire input: \(g(3t)=(3t)^2=9t^2\). Both expressions give the same area at every allowed time.

in²

Worked answer

\(s=3(2)=6\) inches and \(g(6)=6^2=36\) square inches.

Substitution rule

Replace every occurrence of the input variable with the entire new input, using parentheses.

\(g(a+2)=(a+2)^2=a^2+4a+4\). In general, \(g(a+2)\ne g(a)+2\).

Interpret notation without a formula

Exercise 3 · A symbolic dosage model

In this worksheet scenario, \(g\) assigns the amount of a particular medication in one dose (ounces) to a patient’s weight \(x\) (pounds). No formula is given; interpret the notation rather than calculate a dose.

Name the input and output quantities first. Numbers inside parentheses specify a weight; operations outside act on an amount.
3a. \(g(x)\)

Worked answer

The output is an amount of medication, measured in ounces.

3b. \(x\)

Worked answer

The input variable describes the patient’s weight.

3c. \(g\)

Worked answer

The letter g names the relationship from weight to amount.

3d. \(g(145)\)

Worked answer

145 is the input weight, not the output amount.

3e. \(1.5g(85)\)

Worked answer

Multiplication acts on the output. Without a formula, do not assume \(1.5g(85)=g(127.5)\).

3f. \(g(110)+1.5\)

Worked answer

The addition is outside the function, so it changes the output amount.

3g. \(g(195)=2.5\)

Worked answer

An equation pairs the input weight with its output amount.

3h. A solution of \(g(x)=3.25\)

Worked answer

The unknown is the input weight. With no formula or table, we cannot compute it or assume that exactly one weight gives this amount.

Compare two functions

Exercise 5 · Tortoise and hare

A race is 400 meters long. The tortoise starts 225 meters ahead and runs at 2.0 m/s. The hare starts at the starting line and runs at 5.5 m/s. Let t be seconds after the start gun.

Use distance remaining to the finish as the output:

\(g(t)=175-2t,\qquad h(t)=400-5.5t\)

\(g\) is the tortoise’s function; \(h\) is the hare’s. Their separate race domains are \([0,87.5]\) and \([0,800/11]\) seconds.

m

Worked answer

\(g(40)=175-2(40)=95\). At 40 seconds the tortoise has 95 meters left.

s

Worked answer

\(175-2t=80\) gives \(t=47.5\). The tortoise has 80 meters remaining at that time.

s

Worked answer

\(400-5.5t=0\) gives \(t=800/11\approx72.727\) seconds, the hare’s finishing time.

m

Worked answer

\(h(20)-g(20)=290-135=155\). The tortoise is 155 meters ahead: it has 155 fewer meters left.

A difference becomes a new function

\(f(t)=h(t)-g(t)=225-3.5t\). Its graph measures the tortoise’s signed lead. Positive means the tortoise leads; negative means the hare leads.

m/s

Worked answer

The signed lead decreases at 3.5 m/s. The gap shrinks until catch-up, then the hare’s lead grows; the unsigned gap is not decreasing for the entire race.

s

Worked answer

\(175-2t=400-5.5t\) gives \(3.5t=225\) and \(t=450/7\approx64.286\). The runners are level. Equivalently, \(f(t)=0\).

5l. Who wins?

Worked answer

The hare finishes in about 72.727 seconds, before the tortoise’s 87.5 seconds. Also, catch-up occurs before the finish; both are still about 46.429 meters from it.

Change an input, then add outputs

Exercise 6 · Filling a pool

An empty pool receives water from a hose at 2 gallons per minute. Name its cumulative-volume function \(g(t)=2t\), with \(t\ge0\). A second identical hose starts four minutes later.

gallons

Worked answer

\(g(4)-g(1)=8-2=6\). The first hose adds 6 gallons during the interval from minute 1 to minute 4.

Compare your reasoning

One hundred gallons more than the first hose’s contribution after 4 minutes, namely 108 gallons. The expression does not mean 100 additional minutes.

\(f(t)=g(t-4)=2(t-4),\qquad t\ge4\)

6d(i). When does the second hose start, and what is the stated domain of \(f\)?

Worked answer

The input \(t-4\) must be at least zero. At t=4, the second hose has just started and has contributed zero gallons, so the endpoint is included.

6d(iii). How is the graph of \(f\) related to \(g\)?

Worked answer

The same output pattern occurs four minutes later. Subtracting 4 inside the input delays the start.

gallons

Worked answer

\(g(5)+f(5)=g(5)+g(1)=10+2=12\). Together the hoses have added 12 gallons after five minutes.

Domain checkpoint

As written, \(g(t)+f(t)\) is defined only for \(t\ge4\). To describe total water from the first minute onward, explicitly set the second hose’s contribution to zero before it starts:

\(T(t)=\begin{cases}2t,&0\le t<4,\\ 2t+2(t-4),&t\ge4.\end{cases}\)

The simulator uses this total. It never substitutes a negative elapsed time into the original \(g\).

Practice 06 · Polynomial domain

Find the real domain of \(q(x)=x^2-5x+6\).

Worked answer

A polynomial is defined at every real input. Its zeros, 2 and 3, are allowed inputs, not restrictions.

Practice 07 · Denominator restrictions

Find the real domain of \(r(x)=\frac{x+2}{x^2-9}\).

Worked answer

\(x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)\ne0\), so exclude −3 and 3. The zero of the numerator, −2, is allowed.

Practice 08 · Square-root endpoint

Find the real domain of \(s(x)=\sqrt{2x+6}\).

Worked answer

\(2x+6\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-3\). Equality is allowed because the square root of zero exists.

Practice 09 · A root in the denominator

Find the real domain of \(u(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5-x}}\).

Worked answer

The radicand must be nonnegative and the denominator must be nonzero. Together, \(5-x>0\Rightarrow x<5\).

Practice 10 · Combine two restrictions

Find the real domain of \(w(x)=\frac{\sqrt{x+1}}{x-2}\).

Worked answer

\(x+1\ge0\) gives \(x\ge-1\); \(x-2\ne0\) excludes 2. Include −1, where the value is zero, and remove 2.
