01 · Explore the shape

One parabola, several useful features

Change a coefficient or move the vertex. Explain what changes before looking at the readout.

What makes a function quadratic?

A quadratic function has the form

\(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c,\qquad a\ne0.\)

Its graph is a parabola. The sign of \(a\) determines whether it opens upward or downward. The vertical intercept is \((0,c)\).

Vertex form makes the turning point visible.

\(f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k\)

The vertex is \((h,k)\). The axis of symmetry is the vertical line \(x=h\).

  • When \(a>0\), the graph opens upward.
  • When \(a<0\), the graph opens downward.
  • With the same vertex, increasing \(|a|\) makes the graph narrower. A value between \(0\) and \(1\) makes it wider.
  • In \((x-h)^2\), the minus sign matters: \((x+2)^2=(x-(-2))^2\) has vertex input \(-2\).
Why is the graph symmetric?

Inputs equally far from \(h\) give the same output:

\(f(h-d)=ad^2+k=f(h+d).\)

The quantity being squared is the horizontal distance from the vertex.

Try it.

Keep \(h=2\) and \(k=-1\). Compare \(a=1\), \(a=2\), and \(a=-1\). Which features stay in place?

02 · Locate and interpret the extreme

Where is it highest or lowest?

Give both the input where the extreme occurs and the extreme output value.

Start with the vertex

For \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\), calculate

\(h=-\frac{b}{2a},\qquad k=f(h).\)

On the full real domain
OpeningExtreme valueRange
\(a>0\)Minimum \(k\) at \(x=h\); no maximum\([k,\infty)\)
\(a<0\)Maximum \(k\) at \(x=h\); no minimum\((-\infty,k]\)
Worked example

For \(f(x)=x^2-4x+3\),

\(h=-\frac{-4}{2(1)}=2\), and \(f(2)=4-8+3=-1\).

The vertex is \((2,-1)\). The minimum value is \(-1\), attained when \(x=2\). There is no maximum on all real inputs.

A restricted domain changes the question

On a closed interval \([L,R]\), compare the values at both endpoints and at the vertex if its input lies in the interval.

For that same function on \([0,5]\), compare \(f(0)=3\), \(f(2)=-1\), and \(f(5)=8\). The minimum is \(-1\); the maximum is \(8\).

Input versus output

“The maximum occurs at \(x=2\)” names an input. “The maximum value is \(5\)” names an output. These answer different questions.

Why does the vertex determine the extreme?

Since \((x-h)^2\ge0\), \(a(x-h)^2+k\ge k\) when \(a>0\), and \(a(x-h)^2+k\le k\) when \(a<0\). Equality occurs at \(x=h\).

For an upward-opening parabola, the function decreases to the left of \(h\) and increases to its right. A downward-opening parabola does the reverse.

03 · Solve and connect to the graph

Find roots with the quadratic formula

A real root is an input where the output is zero. On the graph, it gives a horizontal intercept.

Put the equation in standard form first

Write \(ax^2+bx+c=0\), with \(a\ne0\). Keep the signs when identifying \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\).

The quadratic formula

\(x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\)

The entire numerator is divided by \(2a\). The symbol \(\pm\) means calculate once with plus and once with minus.

Call \(D=b^2-4ac\) the discriminant.

DiscriminantReal rootsGraph
\(D>0\)Two distinct rootsCrosses the horizontal axis twice
\(D=0\)One distinct root, repeatedTouches the axis at the vertex
\(D<0\)No real rootsDoes not meet the axis
Worked example: \(2x^2+3x-2=0\)
  1. Identify \(a=2\), \(b=3\), \(c=-2\).
  2. Compute \(D=3^2-4(2)(-2)=25\).
  3. Substitute: \(x=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{25}}{4}=\frac{-3\pm5}{4}\).
  4. Separate: \(x=\frac{-3+5}{4}=\frac12\) or \(x=\frac{-3-5}{4}=-2\).
  5. Check each in the original equation. The intercepts are \((\frac12,0)\) and \((-2,0)\).
When roots are irrational

For \(x^2-2=0\), the formula gives \(x=\frac{0\pm\sqrt8}{2}=\pm\sqrt2\). Keep the exact values; use \(x\approx\pm1.414\) to locate them on a graph.

Where does the formula come from?

Divide by \(a\), move the constant term, and complete the square:

\(\left(x+\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}.\)

Taking both square roots gives \(x+\frac b{2a}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2|a|}\). The two signs allow us to write the same pair of solutions using denominator \(2a\), producing the quadratic formula.

Solving for a nonzero output?

To solve \(x^2-4x+3=5\), first write \(x^2-4x-2=0\). The constant in the equation to solve is now \(-2\).

04 · Interpret the mathematics

A maximum height and a landing time

The vertex and the roots answer different questions about the same model.

A ball above the ground

A simplified model for a ball's height is \(H(t)=-5t^2+20t+25\), where \(t\) is seconds after launch and \(H(t)\) is meters above the ground. Follow the ball from launch until it first reaches the ground.

  1. Starting height: \(H(0)=25\) meters.
  2. Time of maximum: \(t=-\frac{20}{2(-5)}=2\) seconds.
  3. Maximum height: \(H(2)=-20+40+25=45\) meters.
  4. Landing: set \(H(t)=0\), giving \(D=20^2-4(-5)(25)=900\).

\(t=\frac{-20\pm30}{-10}=-1\text{ or }5.\)

The negative root refers to a time before launch. For this scenario, the landing time is \(5\) seconds, and the model is used on \(0\le t\le5\).

Always return to the quantities.

The maximum height is \(45\) meters, not \(2\) meters. The roots are times, not heights. Only the root \(t=5\) belongs to the physical domain.

Domain and range for this trip

Domain: \([0,5]\) seconds. Range: \([0,45]\) meters. The height starts at \(25\), rises to \(45\), then falls to \(0\). Both endpoints are included.

Trace the flight

Predict first.

Is the ball higher at \(t=1\) or at \(t=3\)? Use symmetry to explain your answer, then move the slider.

Check the symmetry prediction

Both times are one second from \(t=2\), so \(H(1)=H(3)=40\) meters.

05 · Practice and explain

Put the pieces together

Work on paper first. Check each answer, then open its worked solution. Numeric answers may be decimals.

0 of 10 correct.

Exit question

For \(q(x)=(x-3)^2+2\), explain why the function has a minimum but no real roots.

A complete explanation

The squared term is always nonnegative, so \(q(x)\ge2\), with equality at \(x=3\). Its minimum is \(2\). The output is always positive, so \(q(x)=0\) has no real solution and the graph has no horizontal intercept.
