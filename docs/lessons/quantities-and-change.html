MATH 10675 · Module 2

Quantities & change

What can we measure? What does a variable represent? And how is a value different from the change in that value?

An interactive investigation · Work at your own pace
01 / Identify & define

Is it a quantity?

Definition · Quantity

A quantity is an attribute of an object or situation that you can imagine measuring or counting.

Look for what attribute, of which object or situation, and how a numerical value could be assigned. A quantity can have a fixed or varying value.

An object, a bare number, or a description without a measurement rule is not itself a specified quantity.

Two different checks. First identify a measurable attribute. Then define its variable precisely: what is measured, where measurement begins, which direction is positive (when relevant), and which unit is used. A fixed value can still describe a quantity. A missing unit makes a variable definition incomplete; it does not erase the measurable attribute.

02 / Value versus change

The symbol Δ means “change in.”

A value describes one state. A change compares two states in a specified order. The change is itself a new quantity, measured in the same units as the original quantity.

Δq = qfinal − qinitial
qfinal = qinitial + Δq

Temperature: choose two readings

The sliders start at the scenario’s two readings. Other slider settings are exploration examples.

Values are locations. Change is an arrow.

ΔT is one symbol for a difference; it is not Δ multiplied by T. A negative ΔT means cooling, even when both readings are positive.

Same change, different values: 70°F → 75°F and 80°F → 85°F both have ΔT = +5°F.

Self-check: temperature in Phoenix

Let T be the temperature in Phoenix, in °F. Distinguish a temperature reading from its change: ΔT = Tfinal − Tinitial.

Try these experiments

  • Walking: move x from 0 to 5 feet. Why is the change in distance apart −15 feet, while the final distance is 30 feet?
  • Square: compare x = 1 → 2 with x = 3 → 4. The same change in side length need not produce the same change in area.
  • Reservoir: compare t = 1 → 3 with t = 4 → 7. Then reverse the order of the two measurements.

M2I1 · Exercise 3 · Detailed investigation

The tortoise and the hare

1. Identify the quantities and define variables

The course length (400 m), head start (150 m), and the two speeds (6.5 m/s and 2.5 m/s) are fixed. Elapsed time, each racer’s position, and the gap between them vary.

Let t be time in seconds since the starting gun. Let h and r be the hare’s and tortoise’s distances, in meters, measured along the course from the starting line toward the finish line. The tortoise starts at r = 150, not at r = 0.

Compare your expressions

h = 6.5t     r = 150 + 2.5t

Speed × elapsed time gives distance traveled since the gun. The tortoise’s position also includes its 150-meter head start. Thus r and 2.5t represent different quantities.

2. Explore the race

The diagram straightens the course into a distance scale. For this simulation, each racer stops at the finish line. The hare’s running formula applies until t = 400/6.5 ≈ 61.54 s; the tortoise’s applies until t = (400 − 150)/2.5 = 100 s. After finishing, that racer’s position stays at 400 m.

● Solid green: hare h■ Dashed orange: tortoise r

Position, lead, and distance between

Each graph point pairs a time with a racer’s distance from the start. The vertical gap compares their positions at the same time.

Define the tortoise’s signed lead L = r − h. A positive lead puts the tortoise ahead; a negative lead puts the hare ahead. The distance between them is |r − h|, which is never negative.

While both racers are running, L = (150 + 2.5t) − 6.5t = 150 − 4t. Over an interval in that period, ΔL = −4Δt. This tells how the lead changes; it is not the lead’s value.

3. Reason from the scenario

Use your expressions to answer, then check your reasoning. The slider can help you visualize each result. Round non-integer answers to two decimal places.

See the complete reasoning

At t = 20 s, the hare is at h = 130 m and the tortoise is at r = 200 m. The tortoise has traveled only 50 m since the gun, and leads by 70 m.

They meet when 6.5t = 150 + 2.5t, so 4t = 150 and t = 37.5 s. Both are at 243.75 m, before the finish. At t = 40 s, L = −10 m: the hare leads by 10 m, and the distance between them is 10 m.

The hare finishes at 400/6.5 ≈ 61.54 s. The tortoise finishes at 250/2.5 = 100 s. Therefore the hare wins. When the hare finishes, the tortoise is at about 303.85 m and has not yet reached the finish.

From t = 10 to 20 s, the tortoise’s lead changes from 110 m to 70 m: ΔL = 70 − 110 = −40 m. The final lead is 70 m; its change is −40 m.

The walk to the car

A student starts at a building entrance. Their parked car is 120 meters east along a straight walkway. They walk east at 2 m/s for 30 seconds, pause for 10 seconds, walk west at 1 m/s for 10 seconds, and then walk east at 2 m/s until reaching the car. Treat changes of speed as instantaneous.

Let t be seconds since leaving the entrance, x be signed position in meters east of the entrance, d be distance to the car in meters, and s be total distance walked in meters since leaving. During this trip, 0 ≤ x ≤ 120 and d = 120 − x.

Compare two moments

These interval controls are independent of the trip playback. Look for the A and B markers and dashed change arrows.

Walking toward the car makes d decrease. Walking away makes d increase. Standing still keeps d constant. Total distance walked s never decreases as time moves forward.

Reason, calculate, explain

Compare with a worked explanation

The final position is x = 120 m east of the entrance. The net position change is Δx = 120 − 0 = +120 m. The student walks 60 + 10 + 70 = 140 m in total. Their distance to the car changes by Δd = 0 − 120 = −120 m. The westward detour adds to total distance walked but reduces position x. The final distance to the car is d = 0 m, not −120 m.
