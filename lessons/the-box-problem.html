A box with positive volume requires \(0<x<4.25\).

At \(x=0\), the height is zero. At \(x=4.25\), the base width is zero. The toolbox shows these limiting cases, but neither is a usable three-dimensional box.

Exercise \(2a\)–\(2d\) · Connect the dimensions

Why do we subtract twice the cutout?

Along each original edge, we remove a strip of width \(x\) at both ends. Trace the two orange corner pieces on the paper net.

Length: \(x+l+x=11\), so \(l=11-2x\).

Width: \(x+w+x=8.5\), so \(w=8.5-2x\).

Height after folding: \(h=x\).

Here \(l,w,h,x\) are measured in inches. These names are supplied so we can focus on their relationships.

Which expression gives the base width?

Can this construction produce a base length of \(2\) inches?

The two base dimensions both decrease at a constant rate of \(-2\) inches per inch of cutout. The volume will not have a constant rate of change.
Exercise \(2e\)–\(2g\) and \(3a\) · Model volume

Multiply the three dimensions.

Base area: \(A=lw=(11-2x)(8.5-2x)\).

Volume: \(V=Ah\).

\(V(x)=x(11-2x)(8.5-2x)\)

Length times width gives square inches; multiplying by height gives cubic inches. The moving point on the graph pairs each cutout with its corresponding volume.

Where does volume decrease?

After the peak near \(x=1.5854\), increasing \(x\) decreases volume, up to the limiting cutout \(4.25\). For actual boxes, use \(1.5854<x<4.25\), approximately.

Why is a target of \(70\) cubic inches impossible?

Below the maximum and above zero, there are two physical cutouts. At the maximum there is one. Above the maximum there are none. At volume zero, only the two limiting cases remain.
Exercise \(5\) · Surface area

Count the paper once, not the empty space.

The exterior surface consists of the base and four walls, with no top. Ignore paper thickness and tape. Unfolding preserves their areas.

Add five faces:

\(S=lw+2lx+2wx\).

Or subtract four cutouts from the original sheet:

\(S(x)=11(8.5)-4x^2\)
\(S(x)=93.5-4x^2\).

A correction to the supplied notes

The final worked answer rounds the cutout to \(4\) inches and reports volume \(6\). But \(S(4)=29.5\), not \(25\). Keeping the cutout’s precision gives \(x\approx4.138236\) and \(V\approx2.519287\). Near the boundary, rounding too early can change the volume substantially.
